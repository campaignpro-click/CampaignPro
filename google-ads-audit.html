What is a Google Ads audit?

A Google Ads audit is a structured review of your advertising account. It looks at how your money is being spent, which searches and settings bring leads, and which ones don't, and turns that into a short list of fixes in order of impact. A good audit answers one question clearly: where is my budget leaking, and what should I fix first?

CampaignPro audits Google Ads accounts for free for small and medium businesses in the United States and Canada. The review is walked through with you on a call, so you can ask questions about anything in your account.

What the free audit checks

Every audit covers the same ten areas, because these are where small business accounts most often lose money.

  1. Conversion tracking

    Are leads (calls, forms, bookings) actually recorded, and only once? Tracking that counts page views or duplicate form fills makes every other number in the account wrong, and teaches Google's bidding to chase the wrong visitors.

  2. Search terms

    The real searches that triggered your ads. This is where most wasted spend hides: job seekers, DIY searches, people looking for free options, competitors' brand names or services you don't offer.

  3. Negative keywords

    Whether the account blocks searches that will never become customers, and whether those lists are applied to the right campaigns.

  4. Keywords and match types

    Whether keywords match what you sell, and whether broad match is used with enough conversion data to support it.

  5. Campaign structure

    How budget is split between services and locations. One catch-all campaign usually lets the cheapest, least valuable clicks take most of the money.

  6. Bidding strategy and budgets

    Whether the bid strategy fits the amount of conversion data you have, and whether campaigns run out of budget early in the day.

  7. Location, schedule and device settings

    Whether ads show only where you serve customers (including the "presence or interest" setting), at hours when leads can be handled, and on the devices that convert.

  8. Networks

    Whether Search campaigns are also spending on the Display Network or Search Partners by default, and whether that traffic produces leads.

  9. Ads and assets

    Whether ad copy speaks to what people searched for, and whether call, location, sitelink and other assets are set up.

  10. Landing page match

    Whether the page people land on answers their search, loads quickly on mobile and makes it easy to call or book.

What you get from the audit

  • A clear picture of where your budget goes, including the searches and settings that spend money without producing leads.
  • A prioritized list of fixes, split into quick wins you can change this week and bigger structural changes.
  • A check of your conversion tracking, so you know whether the numbers in your account can be trusted.
  • An honest recommendation. Some accounts only need a few changes you can make yourself. If that's the case, you'll hear it.

Problems an audit commonly finds

These issues come up again and again in small business accounts:

  • Money spent on searches that were never going to convert, for example "jobs", "salary", "how to", "free" or services the business doesn't offer.
  • Conversions that aren't real leads, such as page views, button clicks or the same form counted several times.
  • Search campaigns quietly running on Display or Search Partners, because those options were left on when the campaign was created.
  • Ads shown outside the service area, because location targeting includes people who are only "interested in" the area.
  • One broad campaign covering everything, so the best services and locations never get enough budget.
  • An account nobody has touched in weeks, while costs creep up and the same wasted searches keep being paid for.

Any one of these can mean a large share of the budget is paying for clicks that will never turn into customers.
