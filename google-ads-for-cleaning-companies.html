Google Ads for cleaning companies

How house cleaning, maid service and commercial cleaning companies use Google Ads to win recurring clients, what it costs and how to avoid the most common waste.

By CampaignPro · Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Google Ads is one of the most affordable ways for cleaning companies to get clients who are ready to book. US averages for cleaning and maid services are about $8.50 per click and $47 per lead, with a conversion rate near 18%.[1] The keys are separate residential and commercial campaigns, blocking job-seeker searches from day one, targeting only your real service area and tracking bookings.

Does Google Ads work for cleaning companies?

Yes. People searching "house cleaning near me" or "office cleaning service" usually want to book soon, and they tend to contact the first few companies that look trustworthy. Google Ads puts you at the top for those searches in the neighborhoods you serve. Because cleaning clients often book again every week or month, one new client from an ad can be worth much more than the first job.

Keywords to target and to block

Good cleaning keywords

  • house cleaning [city]
  • maid service near me
  • move out cleaning service
  • deep cleaning service
  • office cleaning company
  • commercial cleaning services

Common negative keywords

  • jobs, hiring, cleaner salary, cleaning jobs near me
  • how to, diy, checklist, tips
  • cleaning supplies, products, vacuum
  • free, cheap (test carefully)
  • car cleaning, dry cleaning, duct cleaning (if you don't offer them)
  • franchise, start a cleaning business

Job seekers are the biggest source of wasted spend for cleaning companies. "Cleaning jobs near me" is searched constantly and looks similar to customer searches, so it needs to be blocked from day one.

Local Services Ads for cleaning businesses

House cleaning companies can also run Google Local Services Ads, which charge per lead and show a Google badge at the very top of results. They're a good complement to Search Ads, which give you more control over which services, areas and searches you pay for.

Tips that make cleaning ads convert

  • Show price or "instant quote" where you can. Cleaning shoppers compare quickly, and clear pricing earns the click.
  • Lead with trust: insured, background-checked staff, satisfaction guarantee, reviews.
  • Send ads to a matching page. A move-out cleaning search should land on a move-out cleaning page with a quote form, not the homepage.
  • Track bookings, not just clicks. Count quote requests, calls and online bookings as conversions.
  • Target your real service area by zip code or radius, so you don't pay for clicks from people you can't reach.

Common cleaning company Google Ads mistakes

  • Not blocking job seekers. Hiring-related searches can quietly take a big share of the budget.
  • Mixing residential and commercial. One campaign means one budget and one message for two very different customers.
  • Too wide a service area. Leads from 40 minutes away are rarely worth the drive.
  • No conversion tracking. Without it, there's no way to tell which keywords bring booked clients.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does Google Ads cost for a cleaning business?

US averages for cleaning and maid services are about $8.50 per click and about $47 per lead. Many cleaning businesses start with a test budget of around $500 to $1,000 per month in ad spend and scale once they know which services and areas bring clients.

Is Google Ads worth it for a small cleaning company?

Usually yes, because cleaning searches convert well and recurring clients are worth far more than their first booking. It's most effective when ads target your real service area, job seekers are blocked and bookings are tracked.

Should residential and commercial cleaning be in the same campaign?

No. Homeowners and businesses search with different words and have very different values, so separate campaigns, ads and landing pages perform better and make budgets easier to control.

What are the most important negative keywords for cleaning companies?

Job-related searches (cleaning jobs, hiring, salary), DIY and tips searches, cleaning products and supplies, and services you don't offer such as car, dry or duct cleaning.

Should cleaning companies use Google Local Services Ads?

They're worth testing. Local Services Ads charge per lead and appear at the top of results, while Search Ads give you more control. Many cleaning businesses use both.
