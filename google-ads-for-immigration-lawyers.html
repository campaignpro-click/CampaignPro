Google Ads for immigration lawyers

How immigration attorneys use Google Ads to get more consultations for family, employment, citizenship and removal defense cases.

Does Google Ads work for immigration law firms?

Yes. People facing a visa deadline, a green card application or a removal proceeding search Google for help, and they tend to contact the firms that appear first and explain clearly how they can help. Google Ads puts your firm there for the case types you want, in the languages your clients speak, and lets you measure which searches become consultations and retained clients.

Advertise in your clients' languages

Many people search for immigration help in languages other than English. Running separate campaigns with ads and landing pages in Spanish (or the other languages your team speaks) reaches clients many competitors miss. Only do this if someone at the firm can handle calls and consultations in that language.

Keywords to target and to block

Good immigration law keywords

  • immigration lawyer near me
  • immigration attorney [city]
  • green card lawyer
  • deportation defense attorney
  • abogado de inmigración
  • work visa lawyer

Common negative keywords

  • uscis phone number, case status, processing times
  • forms, pdf, i-130 form, fee
  • visa appointment, embassy, lottery
  • jobs, paralegal, salary, law school
  • free (test carefully; decide if you offer free consultations)
  • case types you don't handle

Government-process searches (case status, forms, fees, phone numbers) are the biggest leak. They look like immigration searches, but the person wants information, not a lawyer.

Follow the advertising rules

Attorney advertising is regulated by your state bar (or law society in Canada). Avoid guarantees about outcomes, make sure claims can be backed up, and include any disclaimers your jurisdiction requires. Also check Google's policies for legal services ads before launching.

Local Services Ads for immigration attorneys

Google Local Services Ads are available for many legal practice areas, including immigration in many markets. They charge per lead and show a Google Screened badge at the top of results. They work well alongside Search Ads, which give more control over case types and languages.

Common immigration law Google Ads mistakes

  • Paying for government-information searches. Case status, forms and USCIS phone number searches rarely become clients.
  • One campaign for every case type. Urgent removal defense leads need different budgets and response times than citizenship inquiries.
  • Missing language demand. English-only ads miss a large share of people looking for immigration help.
  • Slow response. People with urgent immigration issues contact several firms; the first to respond often gets the consultation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does Google Ads cost for immigration lawyers?

Across all legal practice areas, US averages are about $9.87 per click and $132 per lead. Immigration costs vary widely by city and case type, with removal defense and business immigration usually costing more per lead.

Is Google Ads worth it for an immigration law firm?

Usually yes, because people with immigration needs search actively and cases carry meaningful fees. It works best with campaigns split by case type, negative keywords for government-information searches and fast follow-up.

Should immigration lawyers advertise in Spanish?

If your firm can serve clients in Spanish, yes. Separate Spanish-language campaigns and landing pages reach many people that English-only ads miss. The same applies to other languages your team speaks.

What negative keywords should immigration lawyers use?

Block USCIS and government-information searches (case status, processing times, phone numbers), form and fee searches, embassy and visa appointment searches, and job or law school searches.

Are there special rules for law firm Google Ads?

Attorney advertising is regulated by state bars and law societies, so avoid outcome guarantees and include any required disclaimers. Google also has policies for legal services ads, which should be checked before launching.
