Google Ads for moving companies

How moving companies use Google Ads to book more local and long-distance moves, and what movers typically spend.

By CampaignPro · Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Google Ads works well for movers because each move is a high-value job and searchers are actively collecting quotes. Moving companies with 0–2 trucks spend about $2,780 per month on paid ads on average.[1] The best results come from separate local and long-distance campaigns, blocking truck-rental searches, strong trust signals and fast follow-up on every quote request.

Does Google Ads work for moving companies?

Yes. Moves are high-value jobs, and people searching "movers near me" or "long distance moving company" are actively collecting quotes. Google Ads puts you in front of them when they're choosing. Because each booked move is worth hundreds or thousands of dollars, even a modest number of leads a month can make the ad spend pay back well.

Keywords to target and to block

Good moving keywords

  • movers near me
  • moving company [city]
  • long distance movers
  • local movers [city]
  • office movers
  • packing and moving services

Common negative keywords

  • truck rental, u-haul, penske, budget truck
  • moving boxes, supplies, moving checklist
  • portable storage container brands (if you don't offer them)
  • jobs, hiring, mover salary
  • free, cheap (test carefully)
  • moving car, auto transport (if you don't ship vehicles)

Truck rental searches are the biggest leak for movers: they get lots of volume from people planning to move themselves.

Trust decides who gets the move

People worry about unreliable movers, so trust signals matter more than in most industries. Show your license and registration details (for example a USDOT number for interstate moves in the US), insurance, reviews and clear quote terms in your ads and on your landing pages.

Plan around moving season

  • Summer is peak season. Most moves happen between late spring and late summer, and competition for clicks rises with it.
  • Month-end is busy. Leases end at the end of the month, so demand clusters around it.
  • Use the off-season to win on price. Fall and winter clicks are often cheaper, which is a good time to build reviews and test new areas.

Common moving company Google Ads mistakes

  • Paying for truck rental and DIY searches. These people are planning to move themselves.
  • Mixing local and long-distance moves. They have very different values and competitors, so they need separate budgets.
  • Weak trust signals. Ads without reviews, licensing or clear pricing lose to movers that show them.
  • Slow follow-up. Moving leads request several quotes at once; the first mover to respond often wins.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much do moving companies spend on Google Ads?

According to 2025 State of Moving data reported by SmartMoving, moving companies with 0–2 trucks spend about $2,780 per month on paid ads on average, rising to about $6,137 for 5–10 trucks and $11,984 for 11–20 trucks.

Is Google Ads worth it for movers?

Usually yes, because each move is a high-value job and searchers are actively collecting quotes. It works best with separate local and long-distance campaigns, strong trust signals and fast follow-up.

What negative keywords should moving companies use?

Block truck rental brands, moving boxes and supplies, moving checklists, job searches, and services you don't offer such as portable containers or car shipping.

When should moving companies spend the most on Google Ads?

In late spring and summer, when most moves happen, and around the end of each month. Off-season clicks are often cheaper, which makes it a good time to test and build reviews.

Should local and long-distance moves be separate campaigns?

Yes. Long-distance moves are worth much more and attract different competitors, so separate campaigns and budgets let you control what you pay for each.
