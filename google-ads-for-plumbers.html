Google Ads for plumbers

How plumbing companies use Google Ads to get more emergency, drain and water heater calls, what it costs and how to stop paying for DIY and job-seeker clicks.

By CampaignPro · Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Google Ads works well for plumbers because plumbing searches are urgent and ready to hire. US averages are about $10.49 per click and $129 per lead.[1] The best-performing accounts separate emergency, drain and sewer, and water heater campaigns, block DIY and job searches, track every call and only run emergency ads when someone can answer the phone.

Does Google Ads work for plumbers?

Yes. Plumbing problems are urgent: a burst pipe, a backed-up drain or no hot water sends people straight to Google, and they usually call one of the first plumbers they see. Google Ads puts you in that position for the jobs you want, in the areas you cover, at the hours you answer the phone, and shows exactly which searches produced calls.

Keywords to target and to block

Good plumbing keywords

  • plumber near me
  • emergency plumber [city]
  • water heater replacement
  • drain cleaning service
  • sewer line repair
  • leak detection service

Common negative keywords

  • jobs, apprentice, salary, plumbing school, license
  • diy, how to, youtube
  • parts, supply, home depot, lowes
  • plumbing code, pipe size chart
  • free (test carefully)
  • services you don't offer (e.g. septic, gas lines)

Local Services Ads vs. Search Ads for plumbers

Plumbers can also run Google Local Services Ads, which appear at the very top of results with a Google badge and charge per lead instead of per click. Plumbing is one of the core Local Services Ads categories. Search Ads give you much more control over which services, keywords and areas you pay for. Many businesses get the most leads by running both.

Tips that make plumbing ads pay off

  • Answer every call. Plumbing leads call and hire whoever picks up first. Only run emergency ads during hours you can answer, or use an answering service.
  • Track calls as conversions, counting only calls long enough to be real inquiries.
  • Target your real service area by zip code or radius; long drives eat the profit on small jobs.
  • Mention what builds trust: licensed and insured, upfront pricing, same-day service, reviews.

Common plumbing Google Ads mistakes

  • Paying for job seekers and DIY searches. "plumber apprentice jobs" and "how to unclog a drain" can take a big share of the budget.
  • Running 24/7 ads without 24/7 phones. Missed emergency calls are paid clicks that go to a competitor.
  • One campaign for everything. Cheap small repairs crowd out water heater and sewer jobs.
  • Not tracking calls. Without call tracking, the best campaigns can look like they produce nothing.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does Google Ads cost for plumbers?

US averages for plumbing are about $10.49 per click and $129 per lead. Emergency searches in large cities cost more, while routine repairs and drain cleaning usually cost less.

Is Google Ads worth it for a plumbing business?

Usually yes, because plumbing searches are urgent and ready to hire. It pays off when calls are answered quickly, campaigns are split by service, job-seeker and DIY searches are blocked, and calls are tracked.

Should plumbers use Local Services Ads or Google Search Ads?

Often both. Local Services Ads charge per lead and appear at the top with a Google badge, while Search Ads give you control over which services and areas you pay for.

What negative keywords should plumbers use?

Block job and training searches (apprentice, salary, plumbing school), DIY searches, parts and supply-store searches, code and reference searches, and services you don't offer.

How much should a plumber spend on Google Ads?

Work backwards from the jobs you want: at about $129 per lead on average, 10 leads a month needs roughly $1,300 in ad spend. Many plumbers start with a focused budget on their most profitable service and scale up.
