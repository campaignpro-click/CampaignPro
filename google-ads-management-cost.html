How much does Google Ads management cost?

A plain-English guide to what agencies, freelancers and consultants charge to manage Google Ads, what should be included, and the extra costs to check before you sign anything.

By CampaignPro · Updated September 26, 2026

Short answer

Most agencies charge 10–20% of your monthly ad spend, or a flat fee of about $1,000–$3,000 per month.[1][2] Consultants billing by the hour typically charge $75–$250+ per hour.[2] This is on top of your ad spend, which is paid to Google. Two costs: your ad spend and the management fee

Running Google Ads with outside help always involves two separate costs:

  • Ad spend is what you pay Google for clicks. You set this budget, and it should be paid directly from your own Google Ads account.
  • The management fee is what you pay a freelancer, consultant or agency to build, run and improve the campaigns.

When comparing quotes, always check both numbers, and make sure you know whether a quote includes ad spend or not.

Two things are worth knowing when reading this table. First, at small budgets a percentage looks cheap, which is why many agencies add a minimum fee. One agency in a 2024 comparison charged "$1,000 or 15% of your monthly media budget, whichever is greater".[3] Second, as your budget grows, a percentage fee grows with it even if the work stays the same. A flat fee is agreed upfront based on the work the account needs.

Other costs to check before you sign

  • Setup or onboarding fees. Some agencies charge separately for the initial build. One well-known agency listed setup at $1,200–$5,800 in a 2024 comparison.[3]
  • Contract length and cancellation terms. Minimum terms of several months are common. Check what happens if you want to leave early.
  • Account ownership. The Google Ads account should be in your name, so you keep all the data and history if you change providers.
  • Mark-ups on ad spend. If ad spend is billed through the agency instead of directly by Google, ask whether any margin is added on top.
  • Extras. Landing pages, call tracking, reporting dashboards and other ad platforms are often priced separately.

What should be included in Google Ads management?

Whatever the price, a management fee should cover at least:

  • Campaign structure, keyword research and negative keywords
  • Regular search term reviews to cut wasted spend
  • Ad copy and asset testing
  • Bid and budget management
  • Conversion tracking that records real leads
  • Reporting in plain language: leads, cost per lead and what changed

How much should a small business spend on the ads themselves?

There's no universal minimum. A practical way to set a first budget is to work backwards from the number of leads you want:

Monthly ad budget ≈ leads you want per month × expected cost per lead

Example (illustrative numbers): 20 leads × $40 per lead = $800 per month in ad spend.

Cost per lead varies widely by industry and city, so the first month or two is a test to learn your real numbers. With CampaignPro you can start testing from about $500 per month in ad spend and scale once you see which campaigns bring leads.

Is paying for Google Ads management worth it?

Management pays for itself when the improvements it brings, such as lower cost per lead, less wasted spend and more leads from the same budget, are worth more than the fee. That usually applies once you're spending at least a few hundred dollars a month on ads and don't have time to check the account every week. If your budget is very small and you have time to learn, managing it yourself can make sense at first.
