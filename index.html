Google Ads questions small businesses ask

Is Google Ads worth it for a small business?

Google Ads is usually worth it when people already search for what you sell (for example "emergency plumber near me" or "divorce lawyer"), when a new customer is worth noticeably more than the cost of winning one, and when leads can be tracked. It works less well for products nobody searches for yet, or when the budget is too small to collect enough clicks to learn from. The fastest way to know is a small, well-tracked test.

How are Google Ads management fees usually priced?

There are three common models. A percentage of ad spend, often 10–20%, means the fee rises as your budget grows. A flat monthly fee stays the same as you scale, which is how CampaignPro charges. Hourly billing is less common for ongoing management. Also check for setup fees and minimum contract lengths, which many agencies add.

What does a Google Ads account audit check?

A useful audit checks whether conversion tracking records real leads, which search terms are spending money without producing leads, negative keywords, campaign and ad group structure, bidding strategy, ad copy and assets, location, schedule and device settings, and whether the landing page matches what people searched for. It should end with a prioritized list of fixes.

How can I tell if my Google Ads budget is being wasted?

Common signs are: the search terms report shows irrelevant searches (jobs, DIY, free, competitors), conversions are not tracked or count page views instead of leads, cost per lead keeps rising while lead quality drops, most budget goes to one broad campaign, and nobody has changed the account in weeks. Any one of these usually means money is leaking.

What is a good cost per lead on Google Ads?

It depends on your industry, location and what a customer is worth to you, so there is no single good number. Work backwards: if 1 in 5 leads becomes a customer and a customer is worth $2,000, you can afford well over $100 per lead and still be profitable. A law firm and a restaurant will have very different healthy numbers.

Should I hire a Google Ads freelancer or an agency?

An agency gives you a team and more services, but you often talk to an account manager rather than the person running your ads, and fees are commonly a percentage of spend. An independent specialist is usually more direct and flexible, and you speak to the person doing the work. For most small businesses spending under a few thousand dollars a month, direct access to an experienced specialist matters more than team size.
